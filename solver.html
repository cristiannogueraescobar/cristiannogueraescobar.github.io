How it reads your grid

Plumline looks for one cell that totals the others: that becomes the goal. The cells that total feeds into, and that are currently empty or zero, become the quantities it can change. Any row with a comparison operator and a number becomes a limit. It uses the labels in your first column to name everything, so the answer reads in plain words.

It solves continuous, integer, binary and mixed-integer linear problems: production, purchasing, blending, staffing, transport, budgets, project selection and loading. Use Variable settings to make a decision whole or 0/1 and to set its minimum and maximum. It does not solve genuinely non-linear ones, such as a quantity multiplied by another quantity, and points you to the cell causing the problem.
