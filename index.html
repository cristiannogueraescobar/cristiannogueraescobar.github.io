Plumline

Plumline is a solver for Google Sheets.

It finds the best allocation your spreadsheet allows — how much to make, buy, ship or spend — by reading the model straight out of your formulas, solving it, and proving the answer against your own sheet.

Plumline is an add-on for Google Sheets™, published on the Google Workspace™ Marketplace. It is free.  ·  Privacy policy  ·  Terms of service

What Plumline does

Plumline solves resource allocation problems inside a Google Sheets™ spreadsheet: linear and integer optimisation. You already have the arithmetic in your sheet — quantities, costs, a total, a few limits. Plumline reads it, works out the combination of quantities that maximises or minimises your total without breaking any limit, and writes that combination back into the cells you choose.

It is used for production planning, purchasing, blending, staffing rotas, transport volumes and budget allocation: any problem shaped as "how much of each, given these limits".

Nothing to set up

Open the panel and the model is already there. Plumline works out which cells are the quantities to decide, which total is the goal, and which limits apply — and names them using the labels already written in your sheet, not cell addresses. If it reads something wrong, you correct it; you never have to build it from nothing.

It proves the answer

A solver is one of the few tools whose output you cannot check by looking at it — you asked precisely because you did not know the answer. So Plumline does not ask to be trusted. It writes the solution into your sheet, lets Sheets recalculate on its own, and shows you that the two numbers agree. Every constraint is listed with how much of it you used and how much is left over, so you can see which limit is the one holding you back.

When a model cannot be solved, it says which cell and why, in plain terms: a cell that multiplies two decision variables together, a total that does not move when the quantities move, two limits that contradict each other. It will not return a confident number it cannot stand behind.

Permissions

Plumline can see the spreadsheet it is installed in, and nothing else. Not your Drive, not your other spreadsheets, and no outside service. Google adds two further permissions — your email address and basic profile — to every listing on the Marketplace; neither is requested by Plumline and neither is used by it.

PermissionWhy
View and manage spreadsheets that this application has been installed in To read the formulas of the sheet you are working on and write the solution back.
Display and run third-party web content in prompts and sidebars To show the side panel.

Your figures are never sent to a server of ours, because there is no server of ours. The whole calculation runs inside your own spreadsheet.

What it cannot do

Plumline solves linear problems: totals built from quantities multiplied by fixed rates, added up. That covers most production, purchasing, blending, staffing and budget-allocation models.

It does not solve models where two decision variables multiply each other, or where a condition tests a quantity being decided. Those are genuinely non-linear and no method of this kind can handle them. Plumline tells you when it hits one instead of returning an approximation without saying so.

Quantities can come back as whole numbers when they have to. Tick "whole numbers only" and Plumline searches for the best combination of integers rather than rounding a fractional answer, which is not the same thing and is usually worse. If the search runs out of time with a valid answer in hand, it says the answer is the best it found rather than the best there is.
